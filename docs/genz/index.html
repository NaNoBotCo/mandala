chapter 1

mandala = circle

mandala. MAN-duh-luh. super old word from india. it means circle. that's it, that's the tweet.

monks make them out of colored sand, grain by grain, for days. then they sweep the whole thing away. the most aura-positive move ever.

holy spot in the middle, rings going out, and out, and out.

plot twist: old kings in asia ran their whole country in that exact shape. lowkenuinely iconic.

make one. draw with your finger. it copies itself all the way around.

now sweep it. that's what the monks do. nothing lasts. kings especially. foreshadowing.

chapter 2

your neighbor is your opp

like 2,000 years ago a guy in india named kautilya wrote the og how-to-be-king guide.

he drew it as rings. you in the middle. the kingdom next door wants your land. that's your opp.

the kingdom past them has the same beef with your neighbor. so they're your friend. enemy of my enemy, bestie.

opp, friend, opp, friend, all the way out. this is literally group chat politics.

tap any kingdom. now it's the main character and everyone recolors.

tap any kingdom to make it the main character.

kautilya called it the circle of kings. two thousand years later, still ate.

chapter 3

aura, not borders

back then there were no lines on the map. barely any maps tbh.

a kingdom was like wifi. full bars by the router. two bars in the bedroom. by the mailbox? nothing.

walk far enough and the next king's wifi is stronger. you auto-connect. it's giving switching networks.

some little towns sat right in the middle of two signals and paid both kings. old thai people called that a town under two skies. quiet on the creek behavior. iconic.

and kings fought over people, not land. land was everywhere, people were rare. most followers = the king. it was always about the follower count.

fun fact: the og aura farming clip was a kid dancing on the front of a racing boat in riau, indonesia. old srivijaya waters. the mandala never left.

chapter 4

kings inside kings

every big king had little kings around him. every little king had littler kings. all the way down to the village head guy.

same shape at every size. nesting dolls but make it government.

in north thailand a town was a mueang (MUE-ang), a village was a baan, and the boss was the chao. it means lord.

chapter 5

gifts go up, protection comes down

little king sends the big king gifts. rice. elephants. soldiers. that's called tribute.

it's glazing but with a price tag.

the malay sultans down south sent siam a tiny tree made of gold and one made of silver. bunga mas, gold flowers. the gift that ate. chiang mai sent bangkok gold and silver trees too, every three years.

big king sends back a fancy title, a fancy umbrella, and the message: touch them and you deal with me.

it didn't mean he owned your land. it meant you were his people. big difference.

your turn. you run a little town between two big kings. ten baskets a year. split them. don't get cooked.

when the king dies, everything wobbles

the whole aura came from the guy. not the throne. the guy.

chapter 7

then the mapmakers showed up

1800s. the british come in from one side, the french from the other, with rulers. chat is this real.

they asked: where's your border? siam was like: border? we have a middle.

so siam drew lines fast so nobody would take more. a thai professor, thongchai winichakul, wrote a whole book on it called siam mapped. required reading, no cap.

and when bangkok drew the new boxes, what did it name them? monthon. literally the same word as mandala. the lore is lore-ing.

chiang mai had lords for about six hundred years (not six-seven. six. calm down.), sometimes free, sometimes paying burma, sometimes paying siam. bit by bit bangkok pulled it in. chiang mai's last ruler died in 1939.

hard borders on top of a vibe is the most polyester plot point ever. slide the years and watch the lights turn into lines.

chapter 8

backwards compatible

today north thailand has provinces, districts, a governor, elections. lines on a map. very official.

but go ask a grandma in a village who to see when the ditch floods. it won't be the governor.

she'll say the head man. then the kamnan, the boss of a few villages. then the big house in town, the family everyone knows. thai people literally call it ban yai, big house.

that's the old circle, still running, under the new map. like an old game still playing on a brand new console. tech people call that backwards compatible.

plot twist: the head man and kamnan jobs came from the same 1890s reform that ended chiang mai's own government. new system, same old rings.

nobody deleted the old way. they put the new way on top. that's standing on business.

glossary

words

mandala
circle. a picture of the world with a holy middle. also the shape of old kingdoms.
mueang (MUE-ang)
a town plus the land around it, in thai and lao. big ones had little ones.
chao
lord. the boss of a mueang.
baan
a village. also a house.
tribute
gifts a little king sends a big king to show whose side he's on.
bunga mas
gold and silver trees the malay sultans sent to siam.
merit (bun)
good luck saved up from good deeds. kings needed a lot.
town under two skies
mueang song fai fa: a little town paying two big kings at once. fa means sky, and also lord. sneaky.
kautilya
og indian writer. neighbor = opp. neighbor's neighbor = friend.
galactic polity
professor stanley tambiah's name for it: a big star with little stars around it.
geo-body
thongchai winichakul's word for a country as a shape on a map.
ban yai
big house. a family in a province that everyone goes to.
kamnan
boss of a few villages grouped together, a tambon.
phu yai baan
village head man.
backwards compatible
when the new thing still runs the old thing.
